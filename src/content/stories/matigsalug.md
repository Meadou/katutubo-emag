## Mindanao
@shot: 7.7, 125.0, 6.5, 0.38
**Manobo** is not the name of one tribe. It is a broad name for many related peoples of Mindanao, living from Agusan to Sarangani, in Bukidnon, the Davao provinces and Cotabato, from rugged highlands to the coast.

What the name means is uncertain. One common reading is *Manuvu*, "people". Many groups prefer their own names: the Ilianen, the Livunganen-Arumanen, the Talaandig, the Higaonon, the Ata, the Matigsalug and many more.

On this map we follow one of them, up the Salug River.

- **Manobo**: a broad name for many related peoples of Mindanao, not one single tribe
- **Datu**: a traditional leader, whose standing comes from what he does

"Our tribal name **Matigsalog** is a give away of our origin," the community writes. "It means 'resident from the Salug River'", the river now called the Davao River, "specifically along its watershed."

The Matigsalug live along the banks and tributaries of the Salug, in Kitaotao and San Fernando in Bukidnon, in the Marilog district of Davao City, and in Arakan, Cotabato. They are river people who fish and farm.

- **Matigsalug**: "resident from the Salug River", also written Matigsalog or Matig Salug
- **Salug**: the river now called the Davao River (sources differ on whether it is the river itself or a tributary)
- **Ancestral domain**: about 102,000 hectares recognised in 2003 across Bukidnon, Davao City and Arakan (details to be confirmed with NCIP)

> "As a people we do not comprehend land ownership – only stewardship as a sign of respect to the rightful owners – the spirits."

"The word boundary has no equivalent term in the Matigsalog language," the community writes. A village is made of clans, each led by a datu, who is believed to be *born* with special abilities rather than elected. A good datu is honest, hospitable and even-tempered, and works his own field.

In 1975 the Matigsalug resisted loggers, ranchers and encroachment on their land, in a resistance led by Datu Lorenzo Gawilan.

- **Datu**: a clan leader, believed to be born with special abilities
- **Lulatong**: the communal store, where half of the communal harvest goes

## Kuglung and saluray
@shot: 7.75, 125.2, 0.42, 0.78
@cards: kuglung, saluray, baligen
@figure: player | 0.002, -0.012 | pluck | plays kuglung | face 0.3
@figure: singer | 0.002, 0.012 | pluck | wrap | plays saluray | face -0.3
@figure: v1 | -0.012, -0.004 | listen | face 0
@figure: v3 | -0.012, 0.01 | listen | face 0
@instrument: kuglung | -0.004, -0.012 | face 0.3
@instrument: saluray | -0.004, 0.012 | face -0.3
Matigsalug music often pairs two instruments. A man plays the **kuglung**, a two-string boat lute. A woman plays the **saluray**, a bamboo tube zither, and usually sings. They sing stanzas in turn and dance side by side.

The kuglung is carved from one solid log: a box-shaped body that narrows into a long neck, with a hooked head. One string carries the melody over frets of black beeswax; the other is a drone. The saluray's strings are cut from the bamboo's own skin and lifted on small wedges.

- **Kuglung**: the Matigsalug two-string boat lute, played by men
- **Saluray**: a bamboo tube zither, usually played by women
- **Baligen**: the five-note scale used when the two play together

## The beeswax quarrel

This is the story of **Agyu**, as recorded from the Ilianen Manobo. The Matigsalug honour ancestors with the same names in their prayers, so here those ancestors appear only as light.

At Ayuman, the homeland beside the river, Agyu sent nine lumps of beeswax downriver with his brothers Kuyasu and Banlak, to trade with a datu there. The datu thought it too little. The trade ended in a fight, and the brothers ran home to warn Agyu.

- **Agyu**: the hero of the Ilianen epic, remembered as an ancestor by several peoples
- **Ayuman**: the homeland in the story
- **A note**: the quarrel is about a trade, not about a people

## The fort on Mount Ilian
@shot: 7.77, 125.17, 0.8, 0.66
@cards: Mount Ilian, rattan
@figure: agyu | 0.032, -0.044 | raise | face 0.2
@figure: kuyasu | 0.026, -0.03 | stand | face 0.6
@figure: v1 | 0.022, -0.052 | stand | face 0
@figure: v2 | 0.018, -0.04 | stand | wrap | face 0.4
Agyu foresaw war. He led his people up **Mount Ilian**, where they built a fort.

They gathered logs and boulders and bound them with rattan. When the attackers climbed the slope, Agyu had the rattan cut, and the logs rolled down. The people were safe.

- **Mount Ilian**: the mountain of the fort, near the Pulangi River in the telling
- **Rattan**: the vine that held the logs until it was cut

At Mount Pinamatun the people found wild bees, and Agyu came back from the hunt with a wild pig. The meat and the honey were shared by all.

Mungan, Banlak's wife, was sick. When her share was carried to her, she was found **healed**, and she gave betel nut and young rice for everyone to share.

When the people came home, Mungan was gone. She had gone up to the **skyworld**.

For the Matigsalug, the ancestors who rose to heaven are honoured, and "they are not perceived as dead". So she is shown here only as light.

- **Skyworld**: where Mungan went, in the telling

**Tanagyaw**, Agyu's youngest son, was still a boy when he held off the invaders at the bay of Linayagon.

He turned down a marriage offer because he was still a boy. Later he helped a neighbouring datu, and Paniguan followed him home. Agyu accepted their marriage and gave them Sunglawon.

- **Tanagyaw**: Agyu's youngest son, the boy defender
- **Sunglawon**: the land Agyu gave the young couple

## Nelendangan
@shot: 7.75, 125.25, 1.1, 0.62
@cards: Ulahingan, Nelendangan

In the **Ulahingan** of the Livunganen-Arumanen Manobo, Agyu and his people reach **Nelendangan**, a paradise, and live there in "a paradise on earth".

The Ulahingan is a sacred chant, sung by a *tala-ulahingan* over several days. Here it is only retold in plain words. In 2021 the NCCA published a Filipino translation of the Ulahingan series recorded by Silliman University.

- **Ulahingan**: the epic of the Livunganen-Arumanen Manobo, chanted over days
- **Nelendangan**: the paradise at the end of the journey, shown here as light
- **Tala-ulahingan**: the chanter of the Ulahingan

## Manama and the forest
@shot: 7.75, 125.2, 0.75, 0.66
@cards: Manama, tahikbal, panubad
@figure: v1 | -0.012, -0.012 | listen | face 0.9
@figure: v2 | -0.014, 0.002 | listen | wrap | face 1.4
@figure: v3 | -0.004, -0.02 | listen | face 0.4
@light: 0.012, 0.006
"We believe that **Manama** whom you call God occupies the highest position in the hierarchy of the supernatural," the community writes. Manama is never shown as a figure here, only as light.

Spirits are present in "rocks, water, trees, flowers", and "the forest is a sacred place". The land belongs to the spirits, and people are its stewards. Prayer, the *panubad*, comes before tilling the land, at weddings and burials, for healing and for settling disputes. It is led by a datu or a baylan, and it is not re-enacted here.

- **Manama**: the highest being, called on as "Our Father"
- **Tahikbal**: guardian spirits of people, datus and the land
- **Panubad**: ritual prayer, at the centre of almost all activities

## Craft and dress
@shot: 7.75, 125.2, 0.45, 0.76
@cards: sul-oy, tikus, panday
@figure: smith | 0, 0 | strike | mallet | face 0.4
@figure: v2 | 0.012, 0.01 | stand | wrap | face -2.2
@figure: v1 | -0.01, 0.012 | listen | face -1.4
Men's shirts and pants are "decorated all-over with multi-colored cloth tape painstakingly sewn". Women wear red, orange, blue and yellow. Ornaments include the **sul-oy**, a necklace with earplugs attached, and **tikus**, plant-fibre rings worn below the knee, said to give walking endurance.

The blacksmith's craft, *panday*, is now rare. Smiths pump twin bamboo bellows and forge farm tools, rings and bangles. The community writes that this craft, "like the fate of Matigsalog fabric weaving", may be lost.

- **Sul-oy**: a necklace with earplugs attached
- **Tikus**: plant-fibre leg rings worn below the knee
- **Panday**: blacksmithing, worked with twin bamboo bellows
